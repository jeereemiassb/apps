Page Tree Sorter for Confluence

Sort child pages A to Z, Z to A or by date, and keep the page tree sorted automatically.

Overview

Put the child pages of any Confluence page in order in a few clicks, and keep them in order as the page tree grows.

  • Sort by title, A to Z or Z to A, in natural order: "Page 2" comes before "Page 10", and upper case, lower case and accents do not change the order.
  • Sort by created date or last updated date, newest or oldest first. Handy for meeting notes, release notes and logs.
  • Preview the new order before anything moves. Only the pages that are out of place are moved; the rest stay where they are.
  • Sort all levels below: the children of the page and of every page under it, in one run, with a progress bar.
  • Keep sorted: new pages, pages moved in and renamed pages go straight to their place, for one page's children or for a whole section.
  • Manual sorts run with your own Confluence permissions: you can only move the pages Confluence lets you move.

Getting started

  1. A Confluence administrator installs the app from its Atlassian Marketplace listing.
  2. Open the page whose child pages you want to sort.
  3. Select More actions (•••) → Sort child pages. The first time, Confluence asks you to allow the app to act on your behalf.
  4. Choose Sort by and select Preview.
  5. Check the new order and select Sort now. Keep the dialog open until the progress bar finishes.

Options

OptionWhat it does
Sort byTitle, A to Z (default); Title, Z to A; Created, newest first; Created, oldest first; Last updated, newest first; Last updated, oldest first.
Sort all levels belowOff (default): only the child pages of this page. On: also the children of every page below it, at any depth.
Keep sortedOff (default). On: the app sorts the pages again whenever a page is added, moved in, copied, restored or renamed there (and, when sorting by last updated date, whenever one of them is edited). With Sort all levels below on, the rule covers every level under this page.

The preview shows the new order of this page's children, with their current position and a moves label on the pages that will move. With Sort all levels below on, it also lists the other pages whose children will be sorted and how many pages move under each one.

After sorting, the app reads the order back from Confluence and tells you if anything is still out of place.

Keep sorted

Turn on Keep sorted in the dialog, choose the order and select Sort now (or Save if the pages are already in order). From then on:

  • A page created under that page, moved there, copied there, restored from the trash or renamed is put in its place automatically, shortly after the change.
  • If the rule covers all levels below, it also applies to the children of every page underneath. A page lower down can have its own rule, which then wins for its children.
  • Opening Sort child pages on a page covered by a rule from higher up shows which page the rule belongs to.

To turn it off, open Sort child pages on the same page, switch Keep sorted off and select Save. Only people who can edit the page can turn Keep sorted on or off. Automatic sorting runs as the app, not as a person.

Limitations

  • Only pages are moved. Folders, whiteboards, databases, smart links and archived pages under the page are not moved; the pages are sorted around them.
  • Pages inside folders are not sorted, and Sort all levels below and Keep sorted do not go through folders.
  • Pages at the top level of a space (outside the home page) cannot be sorted: open the action on their parent page instead.
  • Up to 2,000 child pages per page. Sort all levels below reads up to 2,000 pages, or fewer in very deep trees; the dialog warns you when part of the tree is left out, and you can sort the rest from a page further down.
  • Pages you cannot see or move are left where they are; the dialog tells you how many.
  • Keep sorted moves up to 20 pages per change (normally one: the page that was added or renamed). Pages the app cannot see or move, for example because of page restrictions, are left where they are.
  • While Keep sorted is on, ordering pages by hand does not last: the app restores the order the next time a page is added, moved or renamed there. Turn Keep sorted off to order pages by hand.
  • If you close the dialog while pages are moving, the sort stops. Run it again: pages already in place are not moved again.
  • Dates are the ones Confluence keeps: the creation date and the date of the last published version.

Permissions

ScopeWhy
read:hierarchical-content:confluenceList the child pages of a page in their current order, and the pages below it for Sort all levels below.
read:page:confluenceRead the created and last updated dates and the parent of the child pages, and check that you can edit the page before Keep sorted is turned on or off.
read:content.metadata:confluenceRead the ancestors of a page, to find a Keep sorted rule set on a page higher up.
write:confluence-contentMove a page just before or after one of its sibling pages. This is the only change the app makes: it never edits content and never moves a page to another parent.
read:confluence-content.summaryReceive the page created, moved, copied, restored, updated and deleted events that Keep sorted reacts to.
storage:appStore the Keep sorted rules, and a short-lived lock so that automatic sorting waits while someone sorts by hand.

No egress: the app does not connect to any server outside Atlassian.

Data and privacy

The app stores only its Keep sorted rules, in Forge storage inside Atlassian: the page ID, the sort order, whether the rule covers all levels below, the language used for the alphabetical order and the date of the change. While a sort runs it also stores a lock with the page ID and an expiry time, deleted when the sort ends. It does not store account IDs, page titles or content. To work out the order it reads the titles, dates, positions and parents of the child pages. Manual sorts run with the permissions of the person sorting. Logs contain only error codes. No data leaves Atlassian. When a page is deleted for good, its rule is deleted too. See the privacy policy.

Troubleshooting

"Sort child pages" is not in the menu

The action is in the More actions (•••) menu of a page. On a blog post it says "Open this from a page", because blog posts have no child pages. If it is missing everywhere, ask your administrator to check that the app is installed.

Some pages could not be moved

Page restrictions or space permissions do not let you move them. Ask a space administrator, or someone who can edit those pages, to run the sort.

"Confluence still shows N pages out of order"

Someone changed the pages while they were being sorted, or Confluence had not finished saving the new order when the app checked it. Wait a minute, select Refresh preview and sort again.

Keep sorted is on but a new page stayed at the bottom

Check that the page was created directly under the page with the rule (or under a page covered by an all-levels rule), not inside a folder. Pages the app cannot see, for example because of page restrictions, are not moved. The app also waits while someone is sorting that section by hand; the next change sorts it.

I cannot turn Keep sorted on or off

Only people who can edit the page can change it. Ask someone who can edit the page.

The app says it needs an active license

The app is paid and checks the license on every request. Ask your site administrator to check the app's subscription in the administration of Confluence (Apps → Manage apps). During a free trial the app works normally.
